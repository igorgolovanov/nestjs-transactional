# Code of Conduct

## Our standard

This project adopts the
[Contributor Covenant, version 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/)
as its code of conduct. Rather than restating it here, we point at the
canonical text — it is maintained, translated into many languages, and
unambiguous about what it covers.

The short version: be decent to people. Technical disagreement is
welcome and is most of what happens here; contempt for the person on
the other side of it is not.

## Scope

It applies in every project space — issues, pull requests, discussions,
commit messages, code review — and when you are representing the
project elsewhere.

## What this looks like in review

Code review here is direct. Expect a maintainer to say a design is
wrong, point at the specific failure it causes, and ask for a change.
That is not incivility; it is the job. The line is between the work and
the person: "this drops writes when the transaction is read-only" is
review, "you clearly don't understand transactions" is not.

Two habits that keep it on the right side of that line:

- **Argue from the code.** A claim about behaviour is settled by a test
  or a citation, not by seniority or volume.
- **Say what would change your mind.** It turns a standoff into a
  question someone can answer.

## Reporting

Report unacceptable behaviour privately to the maintainer,
[@igorgolovanov](https://github.com/igorgolovanov). If you would rather
not use a public channel and have no private one, GitHub's private
vulnerability reporting on this repository (**Security → Advisories →
Report a vulnerability**) reaches the same person and is visible only to
maintainers; conduct reports sent that way will be treated
confidentially and will not be published as an advisory.

Reports are handled confidentially. You will not be asked to justify
having reported, and reporting in good faith will not count against
you — including when the person you are reporting is a maintainer.

Consequences range from a private word to a permanent ban, in
proportion to what happened and whether it continues.
